--- 1.2 VEHICLE TOWING — WHAT TO DO ---

When police tow your vehicle from a no-parking zone:

Step 1: Find where vehicle was towed
- Ask the traffic officer on site OR
- Call your local traffic helpline (Mumbai: 022-22621855, Delhi: 011-25844444)
- Check city's traffic app (Mumbai: MumbaiTraffic, Delhi: Dilli Police)
- Vehicle is taken to nearest police impound yard / towing compound

Step 2: Documents required to release vehicle
- Original RC (Registration Certificate)
- Valid driving license
- Valid insurance certificate
- Valid PUC certificate
- Your Aadhaar card or any government ID
- If not the owner: authorization letter from owner + their ID

Step 3: Pay the fine
- Towing charges: ₹500–₹2,000 depending on city and vehicle type
- Parking fine: ₹500–₹2,000
- Storage charges: ₹50–₹200 per day after first 24 hours
- Pay at the towing yard or authorized center (ask for receipt ALWAYS)
- Online payment available in cities like Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru

Step 4: Get your vehicle back
- Collect vehicle only after payment
- Inspect vehicle before taking — note any damage on the receipt
- If damage found, complain to Traffic Superintendent immediately

IMPORTANT — AVOID SCAMS:
- NEVER pay cash to individual police officers
- NEVER pay a broker/agent/middleman to release your vehicle — it is illegal
- Always insist on official receipt for payment
- If police demand extra money, note their name/badge number and complain to ACB (Anti-Corruption Bureau)
- Official helpline: 1064 (Anti-Corruption)

--- 1.3 E-CHALLAN SYSTEM ---

- All traffic fines are now digital (e-challan) in most cities
- Check your challan at: echallan.parivahan.gov.in
- Pay online using vehicle number
- If you believe challan is wrong, you can contest it at the Traffic Court
- Un-paid challans can result in vehicle registration renewal being blocked

--- 1.4 LICENSE RELATED ---

Learner's License:
- Apply at: sarathi.parivahan.gov.in
- Fee: ₹150–₹200
- Valid for 6 months
- Must display "L" board while driving

Driving License:
- Apply after 30 days of learner's license
- Fee: ₹300–₹500
- Driving test conducted at RTO

International Driving Permit:
- Available at regional transport offices
- Fee: ₹500
- Valid in 100+ countries

Duplicate License (if lost):
- File FIR at police station first
- Apply at RTO with FIR copy + Aadhaar
- Fee: ₹200–₹500

--- 1.5 ACCIDENT LAW ---

If you're in an accident (Section 134 MV Act):
- Legal duty to help injured person
- Call 108 (ambulance) immediately
- Take to nearest hospital — hospital CANNOT refuse treatment (Supreme Court ruling)
- Inform police within 24 hours
- Do NOT flee the scene — hit and run is a criminal offense under Section 161 MV Act

Third Party Insurance (mandatory):
- Covers injury/death to third party
- Claim through Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT)
- No time limit for third-party claim (Supreme Court ruling 2022)
- Compensation calculated as per structured formula in MV Act

Good Samaritan Law (Section 134A MV Act 2019):
- If you help an accident victim, police CANNOT detain you for questioning
- You cannot be forced to be a witness
- Hospital cannot demand payment before treating accident victim if you brought them

SECTION 2: POLICE POWERS AND CITIZEN RIGHTS
(Code of Criminal Procedure / Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023)
================================================================

--- 2.1 MIDNIGHT / LATE NIGHT ON STREETS ---

Q: Can police arrest you just for being on the street at midnight?
A: NO. Walking on a public street at any time is NOT an offense.

However, police can:
- Ask for your identity (you must identify yourself but not mandatorily show ID)
- Detain you for questioning if they have "reasonable suspicion" — maximum 24 hours without FIR
- Act under Section 151 CrPC / BNSS: preventive arrest if they believe you are about to commit a cognizable offense

Your rights when stopped:
- Ask why you are being stopped
- You do not have to show your phone or unlock it without a warrant
- You are NOT required to go to the police station unless formally arrested
- If arrested, police MUST inform you of the reason (Article 22, Constitution)

Night Patrolling:
- Police have power to check suspicious persons under Section 149 CrPC
- Curfew situations: different rules apply, follow local orders

--- 2.2 ARREST — YOUR RIGHTS (Article 22 + BNSS 2023) ---

When you are arrested:
1. Police MUST tell you the reason for arrest
2. Police MUST inform a family member or friend of your arrest (Section 50A CrPC)
3. You have the right to consult a lawyer immediately (Article 22(1))
4. You MUST be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours of arrest (Article 22(2))
5. You cannot be held beyond 24 hours without magistrate's permission
6. You have the right to remain silent (cannot be forced to confess)
7. Female accused: must be arrested by female officer, cannot be arrested after sunset or before sunrise except in serious cases with magistrate's written permission (Section 46(4) CrPC)

Illegal arrest / wrongful detention:
- File Habeas Corpus petition in High Court
- Complain to District Superintendent of Police
- Complain to State Human Rights Commission
- Complain to National Human Rights Commission (NHRC): nhrc.nic.in

--- 2.3 POLICE SEARCH POWERS ---

Search of person:
- Police can search you if you are arrested
- Cannot strip search in public
- Female must be searched only by female officer (Section 51 CrPC)

Search of premises:
- Police need a search warrant from magistrate for your home
- Exception: if police are in "hot pursuit" of a criminal or believe evidence will be destroyed
- Warrant must specify the place to be searched
- Two independent witnesses (panchas) must be present during search
- Panchas should be from the locality, not police officers
- You are entitled to a copy of the search memo

Your phone / digital devices:
- Police CANNOT access your phone without a warrant
- Unlocking your phone cannot be forced
- However, under Sec 91 CrPC, court can order you to produce documents
- Exception: anti-terror laws (UAPA) give wider search powers

--- 2.4 FIR — FIRST INFORMATION REPORT ---

What is an FIR:
- Official record of a criminal complaint
- Mandatory for cognizable offenses (serious crimes: theft, assault, rape, murder)
- Police CANNOT refuse to file FIR for cognizable offense (Supreme Court — Lalita Kumari case 2013)

How to file FIR:
- Go to police station in whose jurisdiction the crime occurred
- Give written complaint OR oral complaint (police must write it down)
- Read it before signing
- Get a free copy of FIR (your legal right)
- FIR copy is free — do NOT pay for it

If police refuse to file FIR:
- Send complaint by post to Superintendent of Police (SP)
- File complaint before Judicial Magistrate under Section 156(3) CrPC
- File online FIR on state police websites (available in most states)
- Complain to Human Rights Commission

Zero FIR:
- Can be filed at ANY police station regardless of jurisdiction
- Police must transfer it to the correct station
- Introduced especially for rape and serious assault cases

--- 2.5 IF CAUGHT IN A FIGHT / BRAWL ---

What police can do:
- Arrest all parties under IPC Sections 107, 151 (preventive arrest)
- Book for assault (Section 351–352 IPC / BNS)
- Book for affray (fighting in public) Section 159–160 IPC / BNS: fine ₹100–₹1,000

What you should do:
- Stay calm, do NOT resist police
- Do NOT use abusive language with police (can be charged under Section 353 IPC)
- Clearly state if you are the victim
- Ask for medical examination immediately if you are injured (medico-legal case / MLC)
- Contact a lawyer or family immediately
- Police must produce you before magistrate within 24 hours

Bailable vs Non-bailable offenses:
- Simple fight/assault: usually bailable — you can get bail at the police station itself
- Serious assault with weapon: non-bailable — bail from court only
- Bail amount for simple brawl: usually ₹5,000–₹25,000 personal bond

--- 2.6 CUSTODIAL RIGHTS ---

If in police custody:
- Right to food and water
- Right to medical treatment if injured
- Right to meet lawyer
- Right to inform family
- Cannot be kept in custody beyond 24 hours without magistrate's remand
- Remand can be extended to 15 days police custody, then judicial custody (jail)

Custodial torture / third degree:
- Illegal under Article 21 (Right to Life)
- Complain to: State Human Rights Commission / NHRC
- File complaint with court
- File FIR against the officer

--- 2.7 SECTION 144 CrPC (Prohibitory Orders) ---

What it means:
- Prohibits gathering of 5 or more people in an area
- Usually imposed during riots, protests, festivals to prevent trouble

If Section 144 is in force in your area:
- Do NOT gather in groups
- Do NOT carry weapons (even sticks)
- Violation: up to 3 years imprisonment and fine
- Check local news or district website for current orders

SECTION 3: TENANT & HOUSING RIGHTS
(Rent Control Acts vary by state + Transfer of Property Act)
================================================================

--- 3.1 SECURITY DEPOSIT ---

Rules:
- No national law limits deposit amount, but most state Rent Control Acts say 2–3 months rent is standard
- Maharashtra: Rent Control Act 1999 — deposit should not exceed 3 months rent in Mumbai
- Landlord MUST return deposit within 15–30 days of vacating (varies by state)
- Deductions only allowed for actual damage beyond normal wear and tear

If landlord refuses to return deposit:
Step 1: Send written demand notice by speed post or WhatsApp (keep screenshot)
Step 2: If no response in 15 days, file complaint at Rent Control Court (Rent Authority)
Step 3: For amounts below ₹20 lakh: Consumer Court / Small Causes Court
Step 4: Legal notice from lawyer (₹500–₹2,000 for drafting)

--- 3.2 EVICTION RIGHTS ---

Landlord CANNOT evict you without:
- Proper legal notice (15–30 days depending on state)
- Valid reason: non-payment of rent, subletting without permission, illegal use of property, personal requirement by owner
- Court order if tenant contests eviction

If landlord uses force / illegal eviction:
- Call police (it is illegal — Sec 441 IPC / criminal trespass)
- Seek stay order from Rent Control Court
- Landlord can be charged with wrongful eviction

--- 3.3 RENT AGREEMENT ---

Key points:
- Must be registered if tenancy is more than 11 months (Registration Act)
- 11-month agreements are NOT mandatory to register but registering is safer
- Stamp duty: usually ₹100–₹500 for residential + registration fee
- Model Tenancy Act 2021: Central Government template — check if your state has adopted it

What rent agreement must contain:
- Names of landlord and tenant
- Property address
- Monthly rent amount
- Security deposit amount
- Duration of tenancy
- Notice period for vacating
- Maintenance responsibilities

--- 3.4 LANDLORD OBLIGATIONS ---

Landlord must:
- Maintain structural integrity of property
- Provide water and electricity connection
- Not enter property without prior notice (usually 24 hours)
- Not cut off electricity/water supply to force eviction (illegal)
- Not harass tenant in any way

--- 3.5 PAYING GUEST (PG) RIGHTS ---

PG agreements are covered under:
- Indian Contract Act 1872
- Consumer Protection Act 2019 (if services are deficient)

PG owner cannot:
- Impose illegal restrictions (no food from outside, no guests)
- Withhold deposit without valid reason
- Forcibly evict without notice

Complain to: Consumer Forum for deficiency of service

SECTION 4: CONSUMER RIGHTS
(Consumer Protection Act 2019)
================================================================

--- 4.1 CONSUMER COURTS — THREE LEVELS ---

District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (DCDRC):
- Claims up to ₹50 lakh
- File in the district where you live OR where transaction happened
- Filing fee: ₹100–₹5,000 (based on claim amount)

State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (SCDRC):
- Claims between ₹50 lakh and ₹2 crore
- Appeal from District Commission

National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC):
- Claims above ₹2 crore
- ncdrc.nic.in

--- 4.2 ONLINE FILING ---

- File online at: consumerhelpline.gov.in / edaakhil.nic.in
- National Consumer Helpline: 1915 (toll-free, 24x7)

--- 4.3 DEFECTIVE PRODUCTS ---

Your rights:
- Replacement of product
- Refund of full price
- Compensation for injury caused by defective product
- Producer and seller both liable

Time limit to file complaint: Within 2 years of cause of action (now extended by court if valid reason)

--- 4.4 DEFICIENT SERVICES ---

Applies to:
- Banking and insurance
- Housing and construction
- Medical negligence (consumer forum)
- Transport services
- Telecom services
- Hotels and restaurants
- Educational institutions (in some cases)

--- 4.5 E-COMMERCE / ONLINE SHOPPING ---

Your rights under Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020:
- Platform must display seller info, product details, return policy clearly
- Must resolve grievance within 48 hours (acknowledge) and 30 days (resolve)
- Cannot charge more than displayed price
- Mandatory return/refund window as per policy
- Fake reviews are now illegal

Grievance Officer:
- Every e-commerce platform must have a grievance officer
- Must respond within 48 hours

Steps if product not received / defective:
1. Raise complaint on platform app/website
2. Email grievance officer (find email in "Contact Us")
3. Complain to National Consumer Helpline: 1915
4. File on consumerhelpline.gov.in
5. File at District Consumer Forum

--- 4.6 BANKING AND FINANCIAL SERVICES ---

Unauthorized transaction / fraud:
- Report to bank immediately (within 3 days = zero liability for customer)
- Report 3–7 days = limited liability as per RBI guidelines
- Call National Cybercrime helpline: 1930
- File complaint at cybercrime.gov.in

ATM fraud / Skimming:
- Report to bank and police
- Bank must resolve within 90 days (RBI circular)

Loan harassment by recovery agents:
- Recovery agents cannot visit before 7 AM or after 7 PM (RBI guidelines)
- Cannot use abusive language or threaten
- Complaint to RBI Ombudsman: rbi.org.in/Ombudsman

Insurance claim rejected:
- File complaint with Insurance Ombudsman (free, no lawyer needed)
- List at irdai.gov.in
- IRDAI helpline: 155255

--- 4.7 FLIGHT / TRAIN / BUS TRAVEL ---

Flight cancellation (DGCA rules):
- If airline cancels: full refund + compensation ₹5,000–₹10,000 depending on notice period
- If you cancel (24 hours before): refund minus cancellation charges
- Denied boarding (overboarding): ₹10,000–₹20,000 compensation + refund
- Delay more than 2 hours: meals and refreshments mandatory
- Delay more than 6 hours: option for full refund

Complain to: DGCA at dgca.gov.in or AirSewa portal

Train — IRCTC:
- TDR (Ticket Deposit Receipt) for refunds
- File within 30 minutes of scheduled departure for max refund
- Complain at railmadad.indianrailways.gov.in or helpline 139

SECTION 5: WORKPLACE AND LABOUR RIGHTS
================================================================

--- 5.1 WAGES ---

Minimum Wage Act 1948 / Code on Wages 2019:
- Minimum wage varies by state and category of work
- Check your state's minimum wage notification (Labour Department website)
- Maharashtra unskilled: ~₹15,000/month (2024)
- Payment must be on time: 7th of next month for monthly paid workers
- Non-payment of wages: Complaint to Labour Commissioner

--- 5.2 PROVIDENT FUND (PF) ---

Employees' Provident Fund Act 1952:
- Mandatory for companies with 20+ employees
- Employee contributes 12% of basic salary
- Employer contributes 12% (3.67% to PF + 8.33% to EPS pension scheme)
- Check your PF balance: epfindia.gov.in / UMANG app

If employer not depositing PF:
- File complaint at EPFO (Employees' Provident Fund Organization) regional office
- Helpline: 1800-118-005 (toll-free)
- File online at epfigms.gov.in

Withdraw PF:
- After 2 months of unemployment
- For medical emergency, marriage, home loan, education
- Apply online via UAN (Universal Account Number) on epfindia.gov.in

--- 5.3 ESI (EMPLOYEE STATE INSURANCE) ---

- For employees earning up to ₹21,000/month in companies with 10+ workers
- Provides medical care, sick pay, maternity benefit
- Complain about non-coverage at ESIC regional office (esic.in)

--- 5.4 SEXUAL HARASSMENT AT WORKPLACE ---

POSH Act 2013 (Prevention of Sexual Harassment):
- Every workplace with 10+ employees must have Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)
- File complaint to ICC within 3 months of incident
- Inquiry must be completed within 60 days
- Complainant cannot be transferred or victimized
- If no ICC, complaint to Local Complaints Committee (LCC) at District level
- National helpline: SHe-Box (shebox.wcd.gov.in)

--- 5.5 WRONGFUL TERMINATION ---

If terminated without proper cause/notice:
- Industrial Disputes Act 1947 applies to workers
- Notice period: as per contract or minimum 30 days
- Severance: 15 days salary per year of service for retrenchment
- Complain to: Labour Commissioner, Industrial Tribunal

Gratuity:
- Payable after 5 years of continuous service
- Formula: Last drawn salary × 15/26 × number of years of service
- Non-payment: Complaint to Controlling Authority under Payment of Gratuity Act

--- 5.6 MATERNITY LEAVE ---

Maternity Benefit Act 2017:
- 26 weeks paid maternity leave for first 2 children
- 12 weeks for 3rd child onwards
- 12 weeks for adoptive mothers / commissioning mothers
- Employer cannot dismiss a woman on maternity leave
- Crèche facility mandatory for companies with 50+ employees

--- 5.7 CHILD LABOUR ---

Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act 2016:
- Complete ban on employment of children below 14 years
- Complete ban on employment of adolescents (14–18 years) in hazardous industries
- Report child labour: childline.in or call 1098

SECTION 6: FAMILY LAW
================================================================

--- 6.1 MARRIAGE ---

Hindu Marriage Act 1955 (for Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains):
- Minimum age: 18 (female), 21 (male) — child marriage is illegal
- Must be registered at Sub-Registrar's office
- Both parties must be unmarried (bigamy is offense under Sec 494 IPC)

Special Marriage Act 1954:
- For inter-religious marriages
- For those who want civil marriage
- Notice must be given 30 days in advance

--- 6.2 DIVORCE ---

Grounds for divorce (Hindu Marriage Act):
- Adultery
- Cruelty (mental or physical)
- Desertion for 2+ years
- Conversion to another religion
- Mental illness (severe)
- Leprosy
- Venereal disease
- Renounced the world
- Not heard alive for 7+ years

Mutual Consent Divorce:
- Both parties agree to divorce
- First motion petition + 6 months waiting period (can be waived by court)
- Second motion petition
- Faster: 3–6 months typically
- Filing fee: ₹500–₹1,000 at Family Court

Contested Divorce:
- One party files, other contests
- Can take 2–5 years
- Lawyer strongly recommended

--- 6.3 MAINTENANCE / ALIMONY ---

Section 125 CrPC (now Section 144 BNSS 2023):
- Wife, children, parents can claim maintenance
- Interim maintenance during case proceedings
- No time limit to file
- Amount based on income of husband/spouse

--- 6.4 DOMESTIC VIOLENCE ---

Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005:
- Covers physical, sexual, verbal, emotional, economic abuse
- Applies to wife, live-in partner, female relatives
- Can get Protection Order, Residence Order, Monetary Relief, Custody Order in same petition
- Magistrate can issue order the same day in emergency (ex-parte order)

How to file:
- Contact Protection Officer (in every district)
- Go to local police station
- Go to Family Court / Magistrate Court directly
- Women helpline: 181
- NCW (National Commission for Women): ncwapps.nic.in

--- 6.5 PROPERTY RIGHTS ---

Hindu Succession Act (amended 2005):
- Daughters have equal rights as sons in ancestral property (Supreme Court, August 2020)
- This applies even if father died before 2005
- Daughters can claim ancestral property share at any time

Women's stridhan:
- Jewelry, gifts received before/during/after marriage = her exclusive property
- Husband / in-laws have no right over stridhan
- Taking stridhan forcefully = criminal breach of trust (Section 405 IPC)

--- 6.6 CHILD CUSTODY ---

Guardians and Wards Act 1890:
- Best interest of the child is the paramount consideration
- Mother usually gets custody of young children (below 5)
- Father usually gets custody of older boys (but not rule)
- Visitation rights cannot be denied to non-custodial parent

Parental child abduction:
- Taking child out of India without consent = criminal offense
- File complaint with police under Section 363 IPC (kidnapping)

SECTION 7: RIGHT TO INFORMATION (RTI)
(Right to Information Act 2005)
================================================================

What you can ask:
- Any information from Central/State government offices
- Copies of documents, records, contracts
- Status of applications, complaints
- Budget and expenditure records

How to file RTI:
Step 1: Write application addressed to Public Information Officer (PIO) of concerned department
Step 2: Pay ₹10 fee (by postal order, cash, or online for Central Govt)
Step 3: Submit at department office or online at rtionline.gov.in (Central Govt)
Step 4: Response within 30 days (48 hours for life/liberty matters)

Appeal process:
- First Appeal to First Appellate Authority: within 30 days of reply (or non-reply)
- Second Appeal to Central/State Information Commission: within 90 days
- Fine of ₹250/day (max ₹25,000) can be imposed on PIO for delay

Exemptions (what you CANNOT get via RTI):
- Information that threatens national security
- Information received in confidence from foreign governments
- Cabinet papers until decision is made
- Personal information with no public interest

RTI helpline: 1800-11-7002

--- 8.1 TYPES OF CYBER CRIMES ---

Online fraud / UPI fraud:
- Report immediately to: 1930 (National Cybercrime Helpline)
- File at: cybercrime.gov.in

OTP fraud / Phishing:

- Section 67 IT Act: Publishing obscene material online — up to 5 years + ₹10 lakh fine

- Report to platform first, then cybercrime portal

Identity theft:
- Section 66C IT Act: 3 years imprisonment + ₹1 lakh fine
- File FIR + complain to cybercrime portal

--- 8.2 INTIMATE PARTNER DIGITAL ABUSE ---

Non-consensual sharing of intimate images (Section 67A IT Act):
- Up to 7 years imprisonment + fine
- Victim can file complaint directly at cybercrime.gov.in
- Cybercrime helpline: 1930

Online stalking:
- Section 354D IPC / BNS: Up to 3 years imprisonment

--- 8.3 DATA PROTECTION ---

Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023:
- You have right to know what data is collected about you
- Right to correction and erasure of data
- Companies must get your consent before processing personal data
- Data Protection Board to handle complaints (operational soon)

SECTION 10: COMMON SCAMS AND HOW TO AVOID THEM
================================================================

1. TOWING BROKER SCAM
   Scam: Someone near towing yard offers to release your vehicle for ₹500 cash
   Reality: They have no authority. You end up paying both them and the official fine.
   Avoid: Always go directly to the police yard. Pay only at official counter. Get receipt.

2. FAKE POLICE SCAM
   Scam: Person in uniform demands cash for minor traffic violation
   Reality: Traffic challans are now digital. No officer can demand cash on spot.
   Avoid: Ask for official challan receipt. All fines must be digital.
   Report: Badge number + complaint to SP office or ACB (1064)

3. BAIL AGENT SCAM
   Scam: Agent at police station promises quick bail for cash
   Reality: For bailable offenses you can get bail directly from police station free.
   Avoid: Call DLSA (15100) for free legal aid. Know that sureties must sign bond, not pay cash.

4. RTI AGENT SCAM
   Scam: Agent charges ₹500–₹2,000 to file RTI on your behalf
   Reality: RTI application costs only ₹10. You can file it yourself.
   Avoid: File directly at rtionline.gov.in for Central Govt or at concerned department.

5. CONSUMER COURT AGENT SCAM
   Scam: Agent charges ₹5,000–₹20,000 to file consumer complaint
   Reality: Consumer courts have very low fees (₹100–₹5,000 max). You can file yourself.
   Avoid: File at edaakhil.nic.in yourself. Call 1915 for guidance.

6. PROPERTY BROKER SCAM
   Scam: Broker takes advance from both buyer and seller and disappears
   Reality: RERA (Real Estate Regulation Act 2016) regulates brokers and developers
   Avoid: Only deal with RERA-registered brokers and projects (rera.gov.in for your state)

7. JOB FRAUD
   Scam: Company asks you to pay for training, kit, deposit before joining
   Reality: No legitimate company charges employees to join
   Avoid: Never pay to get a job. Report to cybercrime.gov.in

8. LOAN APP HARASSMENT
   Scam: Illegal loan apps charge 300%+ interest and harass borrowers
   Reality: RBI-regulated lenders cannot harass, and interest rates have limits
   Avoid: Only borrow from RBI-registered NBFCs/banks. Report harassment to 1930 and RBI.

SECTION 11: BAILABLE vs NON-BAILABLE OFFENSES (QUICK REFERENCE)
================================================================

BAILABLE (You can get bail at police station itself):
- Simple assault (Section 323 IPC)
- Public nuisance
- Minor traffic violations
- Cheating below certain amount
- Defamation
- Simple theft (first offense)
- Minor drug possession for personal use (in some states)

NON-BAILABLE (Bail only from court):
- Murder (Section 302 IPC)
- Rape (Section 376 IPC)
- Robbery and dacoity
- Serious assault with weapon (Section 326 IPC)
- Kidnapping
- Drug trafficking (NDPS Act)
- Terrorism (UAPA)
- Serious financial fraud

Anticipatory Bail (Section 438 CrPC / BNSS):
- Can apply for bail in anticipation of arrest
- Applied at Sessions Court or High Court
- Useful when you fear false arrest

SECTION 12: KEY CONSTITUTIONAL RIGHTS EVERY INDIAN MUST KNOW
================================================================

Fundamental Rights (Part III, Constitution of India):

Article 14: Right to Equality — No discrimination before law
Article 15: No discrimination based on religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth
Article 16: Equal opportunity in public employment
Article 17: Abolition of Untouchability — practicing untouchability is a criminal offense
Article 19: Freedom of speech, peaceful assembly, movement, profession
Article 20: Protection against arbitrary conviction (cannot be tried twice for same offense)
Article 21: Right to Life and Personal Liberty — most powerful right
           - Includes right to privacy (Supreme Court, Puttaswamy 2017)
           - Includes right to live with dignity
           - Includes right to education
Article 21A: Right to free and compulsory education for children 6–14 years
Article 22: Protection against arbitrary arrest and detention
           - Must be informed of reason for arrest
           - Must be produced before magistrate within 24 hours
           - Right to consult lawyer
Article 23: Prohibition of human trafficking and forced labor
Article 24: No child labor in factories/hazardous employment below age 14
Article 25-28: Freedom of religion
Article 29-30: Cultural and educational rights of minorities
Article 32: Right to approach Supreme Court for enforcement of fundamental rights

Directive Principles (guidance for State policy, not directly enforceable but important):
- Equal pay for equal work (Article 39)
- Free legal aid (Article 39A)
- Living wage for workers (Article 43)
- Uniform Civil Code (Article 44) — not yet implemented

Fundamental Duties (Article 51A):
- Respect Constitution, national flag, national anthem
- Protect sovereignty and integrity of India
- Promote harmony and spirit of brotherhood
- Protect environment and wildlife
- Develop scientific temper
